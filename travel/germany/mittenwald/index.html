Find your ideal stay in Mittenwald

Mittenwald is a colorful Bavarian Alpine town famous for Lüftlmalerei-painted houses, violin-making heritage and the surrounding Karwendel mountains. Its compact center, rail connections and outdoor attractions make it an excellent base.

Best for first-time visitors

Mittenwald Old Town

The historic center is the most convenient choice for restaurants, cafés, shops and Mittenwald's famous painted façades. First-time visitors can easily walk to St. Peter and Paul Church, the violin-making museum and the station.

Why stay in Mittenwald?

Mittenwald combines a storybook Bavarian Old Town with frescoed houses, centuries of violin-making heritage and direct access to the Karwendel Alps. Staying several nights gives travelers time for the village, mountain trails, nearby lakes and scenic day trips without rushing.

Mittenwald travel tips

Book Summer and Winter Stays Early

Mittenwald becomes busier during summer hiking months, Christmas markets and popular winter weekends. Compare hotel availability early if you want a central room or a stay with an unobstructed mountain view.

Use the Station for Car-Free Trips

The area near Mittenwald station is practical for travelers without a car. Direct regional trains connect with Garmisch-Partenkirchen, Munich and Innsbruck, while local buses provide access to nearby lakes and Alpine villages.

Combine Culture and Nature

Beyond the painted Old Town, allow time for the violin-making museum, Karwendelbahn, Leutasch Gorge and nearby Lautersee. Comfortable shoes and layered clothing are useful throughout the year.

Mittenwald FAQ

Is Mittenwald worth visiting?

Yes. Mittenwald combines colorful Lüftlmalerei houses, violin-making heritage, Alpine scenery, hiking and easy access to the Karwendel mountains.

Where is the best place to stay in Mittenwald?

The Old Town is best for first-time visitors because it is close to restaurants, painted houses and cultural sights. The station area suits car-free travelers, while the Karwendel side offers easier mountain access.

How many days do you need in Mittenwald?

Two days are enough for the Old Town and one mountain excursion. Three days allow time for the Karwendel cable car, violin-making museum, Lautersee and nearby Alpine villages.

How do you get to Mittenwald?

Regional trains connect Mittenwald directly with Garmisch-Partenkirchen and Munich, while services south continue toward Innsbruck. Local buses also connect nearby lakes and Alpine villages.
