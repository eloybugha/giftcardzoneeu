Kyoto to Kiyomizu-dera: Bus or Train via Kiyomizu-Gojo?

Quick answer: a shorter walk by bus, or rail plus a longer approach

From Kyoto Station, compare a bus to Gojo-zaka. If you are already near Keihan, consider Kiyomizu-Gojo and the walk uphill. Neither option takes you straight to the temple entrance.

Starting at Kyoto Station?

A direct bus avoids a rail transfer. Check the destination and route: regular City Bus 206 and the sightseeing express services have different fares and operating arrangements.

Starting near Gion or Keihan?

Compare walking from your actual location with taking a train to Kiyomizu-Gojo. Going to Kyoto Station first may add an unnecessary detour.

Think beyond the station name. Kiyomizu-Gojo is a Keihan station, not a station inside the temple grounds. Include the approach walk in your itinerary.

From Kyoto Station by bus

Regular City Bus 206

Use the Kyoto Station bus terminal signs for a 206 service traveling via Higashiyama toward Gojo-zaka. Confirm the direction before joining the queue; the route number alone is not enough. Alight at Gojo-zaka and follow the signed temple approach.

Do not schedule a tight connection around a city-bus estimate. Traffic, boarding queues and a crowded approach can all extend the trip. Leave space in your plan before a timed booking.

Sightseeing express EX100 / EX101

On operating days, these services offer a direct sightseeing connection from Kyoto Station to Gojo-zaka. The adult single fare is ¥500. The operator also accepts the Subway & Bus 1-Day Pass without an extra express charge.

The advertised ride time ends at the bus stop. You still need to walk uphill to the temple. Check the current departure stand, operating hours and return stops on the official route page.

Via Keihan Kiyomizu-Gojo

Choose a Keihan service that explicitly lists Kiyomizu-Gojo as a stop. Save the operator name with the station: subway Gojo is a different station and gives you a different approach.

From Kiyomizu-Gojo, allow approximately 25 minutes to reach the temple on foot. Give yourself more time if you expect to stop along the way. Comfortable shoes matter more here than shaving a minute off the train journey.

What if I start at Kyoto Station?

There is no direct Keihan departure from Kyoto Station. If you prefer rail, search a complete itinerary to Kiyomizu-Gojo, including transfers and walking. Compare the total journey with the bus instead of comparing only the time spent on a train.

Already in Gion? Decide whether you want a sightseeing walk to the temple or a shorter bus approach. Traveling to another station first is not automatically the most convenient choice.

The uphill walk and accessible entrance

Follow the temple’s official access map for the final approach. It identifies access via Kiyomizu-zaka to Nio-mon, or via the entrance at the top of Chawan-zaka. It also cautions that some map-app routes do not actually lead into the grounds.

If you need step-free access, consult the temple’s wheelchair map before choosing your route. The temple describes barrier-free routes for wheelchair and stroller users; the surrounding sightseeing streets still need separate planning.

Plan the return as well. A downhill sightseeing walk and a direct return to your transport stop are different plans. Choose one before adding extra stops.

Temple admission, hours and visit length

Adult admission: ¥500

Use ¥500 as the regular admission reference. This is separate from transport, meals and any optional activity. Confirm current entry conditions before visiting.

Opening starts at 6:00 a.m.

Closing times vary by season and event. Use the official calendar for your date; special evening openings do not mean the temple stays open late every night.

How much time should you set aside?

Our planning suggestion is 60–90 minutes for the temple itself, with extra time for entry queues, photographs and a slower pace. For the temple plus surrounding streets and a break, set aside roughly 3–4 hours, excluding travel from your hotel. These are flexible allowances, not official tour durations.

Begin with the main hall and wooden stage, then follow the permitted visitor route. Avoid trying to fit every nearby sight into a short stop.

A suggested Kiyomizu-dera–Gion walk

  1. Arrive with time for the ascent. Start from your chosen bus stop or station and keep the temple’s closing time in mind.
  2. Visit Kiyomizu-dera first. Leave shopping until afterward if you want to protect your temple time.
  3. Continue through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka. Treat the streets as part of the outing, with time for steps, browsing and a seated break.
  4. Head toward Yasaka Shrine and Gion. Continue only as far as your energy and remaining daylight allow.
  5. Return from your finishing point. Compare transport near Gion or Shijo Kawaramachi instead of automatically walking back to your arrival stop.

This is a suggested sequence, not a timed guided tour. Shops and other attractions have their own hours and admission conditions.

Practical tips for the visit

  • Travel light. Slopes and crowded streets are easier to manage without a large suitcase.
  • Keep paths clear. Pause for photographs where others can pass, and respect private entrances.
  • Follow temple photography rules. Drones, tripods and monopods are prohibited; commercial recording requires permission.
  • Use the accessible map when needed. The scenic street route is not a substitute for checking step-free access.
  • Keep the day flexible. Shorten the onward walk if queues or weather have used up your time.

Kyoto to Kiyomizu-dera FAQ

Is there a train station at Kiyomizu-dera?

No station is at the temple entrance. Keihan Kiyomizu-Gojo is an access option, followed by approximately 25 minutes on foot, including an uphill approach.

Which bus can I take from Kyoto Station?

Compare City Bus 206 toward Higashiyama with sightseeing express EX100 or EX101 on operating days. Alight at Gojo-zaka and continue uphill on foot.

Are all Kyoto buses 230 yen?

No. The ordinary City Bus flat fare is ¥230 for adults, while sightseeing express EX100 and EX101 cost ¥500 per adult ride. Other operators and routes can have different fares.

How much is Kiyomizu-dera admission?

Budget ¥500 per adult for regular temple admission. Transport, optional purchases and separately ticketed experiences are additional; confirm current entry conditions before visiting.

Can I visit Kiyomizu-dera at night?

Only plan an evening visit after checking the official calendar. Opening starts at 6:00 a.m., but closing times and special night-viewing dates vary.

Can I combine Kiyomizu-dera with Gion?

Yes. A suggested outing starts at the temple, continues through Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka, then heads toward Yasaka Shrine and Gion. Allow extra time for slopes, steps, breaks and crowds.
