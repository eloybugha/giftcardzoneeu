Tokyo to Nara via Kyoto: JR Nara or Kintetsu-Nara for Nara Park?

Both routes start with the same Shinkansen to Kyoto. The train you choose next determines where you step out in Nara and how far you carry your bags.

Quick answer for Nara Park

Take the Shinkansen to Kyoto, then look for a direct Kintetsu Express to Kintetsu-Nara. The ordinary express costs ¥760. From Kintetsu-Nara, allow about eight minutes on foot to the near side of the park.

If your hotel is beside JR Nara, or you have a Japan Rail Pass that covers the JR leg, consider the JR Miyakoji Rapid. It costs ¥720 on its own and leaves you about 20 minutes’ walk from the park. For Tōdai-ji or another sight inside the park, map the walk to the entrance you intend to use.

Time and budget from Tokyo

A Nozomi from Tokyo Station to Kyoto takes about 2 hours 20 minutes. A Hikari takes about 2 hours 40 minutes. An ordinary non-reserved Tokyo–Kyoto ticket costs about ¥13,320 when that seat type is available.

Example with separate tickets. A ¥13,320 Tokyo–Kyoto ticket plus a ¥760 Kintetsu Express ticket comes to ¥14,080 per adult. Choosing the reserved Kintetsu Limited Express brings that example to ¥14,600. For an all-JR itinerary, ask JR to price the journey through to Nara. Online offers, reserved seats and travel to Tokyo Station can change your total.

For a rough park arrival time, add 140 minutes on the Nozomi, 46 minutes on a direct Kintetsu Express and eight minutes on foot. That gives 3 hours 14 minutes before the walk between platforms and the wait in Kyoto. Use the next train you can actually board when planning your day.

How to change at Kyoto Station

  1. For JR Nara, follow signs for the JR conventional lines after leaving the Shinkansen platform. Look for the Nara Line and the Miyakoji Rapid bound for Nara. A local train makes more stops.
  2. For Kintetsu-Nara, follow signs for Kintetsu Railway and use its gates with your onward fare. Check the destination on the departure board. If your itinerary calls for a change at Yamato-Saidaiji, save both train details.
  3. With luggage, allow time to find lifts, buy the onward ticket and reach the platform. If you plan to leave bags at a hotel, compare the walk to reception before choosing your Nara station.

Booking online? Check the departure station, arrival station, seat type and boarding instructions. Use the railway ticket machines or counter in Kyoto if the onward product you need is missing from the booking site.

Tokyo to Nara FAQ

Is there a direct Shinkansen from Tokyo to Nara?

Change trains at Kyoto. The Tokaido Shinkansen brings you to Kyoto, where you can continue to Nara by JR or Kintetsu.

Do I need to reserve the Kyoto to Nara train?

Ordinary Kintetsu Express and JR Miyakoji Rapid travel does not require a seat reservation. For the Kintetsu Limited Express, buy the reserved-seat ticket in addition to the regular fare.

Which station should I use with a suitcase?

If you are staying overnight, check which station is closer to your hotel and whether reception will hold your bag. If the park is your first stop, Kintetsu-Nara gives you the shorter walk.

Will my Tokyo to Kyoto booking cover Nara?

Read the destination on the ticket. A Tokyo–Kyoto booking ends at Kyoto. If your JR ticket already covers onward travel to JR Nara, check its conditions before buying another fare. Kintetsu is a separate railway.
