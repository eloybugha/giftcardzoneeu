Kyoto to Uji Day Trip: JR or Keihan for Byodoin & Matcha?

Start with the railway closest to your Kyoto base. Travel directly from Kyoto Station with JR, or use Keihan from eastern Kyoto via Chushojima, then explore Byodoin, the river and Uji’s tea streets.

Quick answer: JR from Kyoto Station, Keihan from eastern Kyoto

Near Kyoto Station? Take the JR Nara Line to JR Uji for a direct journey. Near Gion-Shijo or Sanjo? Compare Keihan to Chushojima, then change to the Uji Line for Keihan Uji.

Direct from Kyoto Station

JR avoids a railway change. Check the service type: a rapid train is quicker than an all-stops local.

Starting near the Kamogawa

Keihan can save a detour to Kyoto Station. Include the connection at Chushojima when comparing journeys.

Where to board and change trains

JR Kyoto: follow the Nara Line signs

Enter the conventional JR gates and check the Nara Line departure board for a train stopping at Uji. Confirm the platform and service type rather than relying on a fixed platform number.

Keihan: change at Chushojima

From your Keihan station in Kyoto, take a suitable southbound service to Chushojima. Follow signs for the Uji Line and board for Uji. Stay on to the terminus; do not continue on the Main Line toward Osaka.

Starting at Kyoto Station but considering Keihan?

A Keihan itinerary needs additional access or transfers because its Main Line does not depart from Kyoto Station. Use the direct JR option as your first comparison, then consider Keihan if another stop or an existing pass makes it useful.

JR Uji or Keihan Uji: the walk to Byodoin

From JR Uji

Follow signs toward central Uji and Byodoin. The temple’s official access guide gives about a 10-minute walk from JR Uji Station.

From Keihan Uji

Arrive near Uji Bridge on the river’s east side. Cross toward the temple side and continue to Byodoin; the official guide also gives about 10 minutes on foot.

Allow longer for crossings, photos or a slower walking pace. These are different stations, so save the correct one for the journey back.

Suggested half-day: temple, river and tea

  1. Travel to Uji in the morning. Choose JR or Keihan around your accommodation and leave time for the walk from the station.
  2. Visit Byodoin first. Explore the garden and museum, and enquire about Phoenix Hall interior availability if you want to go inside.
  3. Leave time for a tea stop. Browse the approach streets and choose a cafe or shop around its menu, opening hours and queue. For a tea-making experience, check whether advance booking is needed.
  4. Walk beside the Uji River. Add a short riverside stroll or cross toward the shrine area if time and energy allow.
  5. Return or extend your visit. Head back to your chosen station, or keep more time for lunch and another stop.

This is a suggested order, not a fixed timetable. A relaxed half-day can become a full day with queues, a longer tea experience or extra sightseeing.

Returning to Kyoto

For Kyoto Station, use JR Uji and a suitable Kyoto-bound Nara Line service. For Gion-Shijo or Sanjo, use Keihan Uji, change at Chushojima and continue on a suitable northbound Main Line train.

You can return with a different operator if you pay separately or have valid coverage. Check the actual return journey before leaving central Uji, especially after an evening meal.

Kyoto to Uji FAQ

Should I take JR or Keihan from Kyoto to Uji?

From Kyoto Station, JR is the straightforward direct route. From around Gion-Shijo or Sanjo, compare Keihan via Chushojima so you do not first have to travel to Kyoto Station.

Are JR Uji and Keihan Uji the same station?

No. They are separate stations. Both give access to central Uji, but Keihan Uji is beside the river near Uji Bridge. Save the correct station for your return.

Where do I change for the Keihan Uji Line?

Change at Chushojima from the Keihan Main Line to the Uji Line, then travel to its Uji terminus. Check the departure displays for your platform.

Does Byodoin admission include the Phoenix Hall interior?

The standard adult garden and Hoshokan museum ticket is ¥700. A Phoenix Hall interior visit costs an additional ¥300 and is subject to availability; obtain its ticket at the reception inside the garden.

Can I visit Uji in half a day?

A half-day can work for Byodoin, a riverside walk and a tea stop. Allow longer for queues, a tea experience or extra sightseeing, and check the last entry times.

Do I need to reserve an ordinary train seat?

Ordinary JR Nara Line and Keihan seating does not require a reservation and a seat is not guaranteed. Separately priced premium seating products have their own conditions.
